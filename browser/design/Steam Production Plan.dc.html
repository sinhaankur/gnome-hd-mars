Mars HAWC · Production plan26 November 2026

From browser prototype to Steam Early Access in Godot

This plan assumes a team of 4 to 8, a six-month runway, Early Access first and then 1.0, and Windows, Linux, macOS and Steam Deck builds. All six art and audio areas are outsourced. Your form ticked every outsourcing option as well as "Nothing, all in-house", so I've treated the outsourcing answers as the intent. Valve rules and dates are cited. Schedules, team splits and scope cuts are my recommendations.

The scope call

The full scope fits across Early Access, not on day one

The stated scope is 20+ missions, four vehicle classes, skirmish, PvP and four platforms. Six months with a team of up to eight cannot produce all of that to Steam quality. Early Access exists for this case. Valve warns against launching with only a tech demo and against launching a game that is already finished, because Early Access is where players shape the final game (Valve EA rules, as quoted).

So the Early Access launch build carries the whole hijack loop and all four classes, since that is the core fantasy. The campaign and PvP grow in public after launch.

02 · Steam requirements

Valve's gates, and how long each takes

  • You can't release until 21 days after paying the app fee (Steamworks onboarding).
  • A Coming Soon page has to be public for at least two weeks before release, Early Access included (Steamworks release options). Submit the page for review at least 7 business days before you want it live (Coming Soon docs).
  • The release date can't change within two weeks of launch (release dates docs).
  • Sources disagree on build review time. Valve's onboarding page says 1–5 days. A November 2026 guide reports that Valve says reviews are taking up to 12 business days (AI Game Dev). The plan assumes the longer figure.
  • If an Early Access update will break save files, Valve asks you to tell players up front. The Early Access price must be no higher than on any other store.
Steam Deck Verified
  • Every menu works with a controller, and the prompts show the correct button icons. Text entry opens the on-screen keyboard. No launcher (A+ Games).
  • The smallest text is at least 9 px tall at 1280×800, and Valve recommends 12 px (Envy).
  • Frame rate: one guide says 30 fps at 800p with default settings (GamingOnLinux), another says 25 fps (GTSTU). The plan targets 40 fps with a dedicated Deck preset.
  • Godot's official Linux export runs on the Deck natively (Godot docs).
03 · Godot standards

Pin one engine version and keep every change shippable

  • Engine. The latest stable release is 4.7.2 (August 2026), and 4.8 is still in beta (Godot Awesome). W4 Consoles lists support for 4.4 to 4.6 (W4 Games). Since consoles come later, check W4's 4.7 support before locking the version, or stay on 4.6.x. After that, take only maintenance releases.
  • Physics. Use Jolt, which has been Godot's default since 4.6 (Ziva). All four vehicle classes run on it.
  • Architecture. The pilot is its own persistent node that moves between hulls and is never destroyed with them. Each hull scene is built from components: seat, cell bay, damage model, weapons and locomotion. Class stats live in .tres resources so designers can tune them without touching code. Damage disables components rather than killing the vehicle, so a hull can still be captured.
  • Code. Typed GDScript, with untyped-variable warnings set to errors. C++ GDExtension only where profiling demands it, such as MOLA terrain streaming. Unit tests for the hijack, damage and cell economy rules.
  • Assets. Export from Blender as .glb: real metres, feet at y=0, and 30k triangles or fewer for enemies. Store binaries in Git LFS. Use shared import presets with automatic LODs.
  • Builds. On every push, CI exports headless Windows, Linux and macOS builds. A nightly SteamPipe upload goes to a private beta branch. macOS builds need an Apple Developer account for signing and notarisation.
Steam integration and PvP

GodotSteam is an open-source Steamworks module for Godot 4 on Windows, Linux and Mac (GodotSteam). It covers achievements, cloud saves, Steam Input, rich presence and lobbies. Its MultiplayerPeer, which connects Godot's multiplayer nodes to Steam networking, has been merged into the main 4.x branch (Codeberg). Godot doesn't ship a relay of its own, and for Steam builds GodotSteam is the most proven option (Ziva).

The hardest PvP problem is the hijack itself. The moment a pilot boards an enemy hull, network ownership of that hull has to pass to the new pilot. That's why PvP gets its own prototype from April and ships in Update 1.

05 · Rendering and visual quality

Visual quality is checked against references, not judged by eye

Three people own the image: the core technical artist, a rendering engineer and an environment and lighting artist. Explosion and dust VFX packs and hero-hull texturing are outsourced to specialists. The pipeline choices below are my recommendations for Godot.

  • Renderer. Forward+ on Vulkan for every platform, so PC and Deck share one look and differ only in presets.
  • Lighting. On PC, real-time global illumination (SDFGI) across the open terrain. Baked lightmaps inside bases. On Deck, sky ambient plus SSAO. One physical sun drives the real day/night cycle at every site.
  • Atmosphere. Volumetric fog for dust storms and distance haze. The sky shader's butterscotch colour is checked against Curiosity and Perseverance frames.
  • Image quality. TAA on PC. On Deck, the scene renders below 800p and Godot's built-in FSR 2 upscales it. HDR output for supported displays, which arrived in Godot 4.7 (Ziva).
  • No shader stutter. Use the shader baker added in Godot 4.5 (Ziva), so shaders are compiled before the first mission instead of hitching mid-fight.
  • Materials. Physically based materials with consistent texel density. Targets: 512 px/m on hero hulls, 256 px/m on props. Regolith and rock layers are built from HiRISE and rover imagery, with decals for tracks, scorch and dust.
Quality gate, weekly
  • Each mission site gets a reference board of rover frames at a matching sun angle.
  • Every changed shot is screenshotted beside its reference frame.
  • Sky colour, exposure and regolith tone are checked with a colour picker against the reference.
  • The art director gives the verdict first. "Looks fine" doesn't pass.
  • GPU time for each changed shot is profiled on the Deck and on a min-spec PC.

06 · Flight

Cockpit dogfights that end in a hijack

Flight missions draw on the classic space-combat-sim tradition: a cockpit view, close dogfights and managing your power under fire. They have to stay hard sci-fi, though, and they have to feed the hijack loop. These rules are my recommendations.

  • Where flight happens. Mars's air is about 1% as dense as Earth's at sea level, so winged dogfights low over the surface wouldn't be credible. Flight happens in orbit around the Ares Ring and Phobos, and in rocket-powered descents and ascents between orbit and the surface.
  • Flight model. Newtonian, with a flight-assist toggle. With assist on, damping makes it easy to fly on a controller or Deck. With it off, you get true inertia for experts.
  • Power split. The pilot divides the methalox cell's output between thrust, guns and armour. These are the same cells as on the ground, so a hit to the cell bay matters in the air too.
  • Hijack in orbit. Knock out a fighter's engines without destroying it, match its velocity and eject. Cross to it with the jetpack, force its pilot out with GASHR and take the craft. Flight becomes the core fantasy in a new place, not a separate game.
Act 3, the gamble
  1. Descent escort. Cover a Starship-class tanker's powered descent to your base while Starfall interceptors attack it.
  2. Phobos intercept. Catch a Darken courier near Phobos and disable it so it can be captured.
  3. Ares Ring boarding. Fight your way to the dock, cross the hull on foot and escape in the station's own fighter. This sets up the reversal.

The supply chain gives you a reason to fly

Mining feeds a chain of rovers and ships that runs to a timetable in every mission. You can see landers launch and land on the horizon even during ground missions. Each link in the chain can be attacked and hijacked, and cutting a link lowers that faction's cell stock on the war map.

07 · Population and control

Every base is a town, and someone runs it

People keep the supply chain running. Each base has a fictional population living in regolith-shielded habs and working shifts: miners, rover drivers, plant technicians, pad crews, traffic controllers and medics. These systems are my recommendations.

  • Crew drives output. A base makes cells at a rate set by the crew on shift, the plant's capacity and the depth of its ice. Scatter the crew and the chain slows. When you capture a base, its crew either works for you or refuses, depending on morale.
  • Control towers. Each tower sets the base's convoy timetable and lander slots. Take the tower and you can send a convoy into an ambush or hold a tanker on the pad. In effect you hijack the schedule as well as the hull.
  • Life support. The same Sabatier and electrolysis plant makes the oxygen people breathe, so cutting it has a human cost. Crews have a few hours of reserve, and the game shows what follows: distress calls and evacuation rovers. Nobody cheers.
  • Non-combatants. Civilians don't fight. They shelter, evacuate or surrender. Pilots who eject survive as the same person, because the rule is capture, not kill. Hitting habs costs you reputation with every faction and changes how bases receive you.
  • Control map. The war map shows who controls each base and how many people live there. A base changes hands when you take its tower and cell dock, not when everyone in it is dead.
What the player sees
  • Crews walking between habs at shift change
  • Lit hab windows at night
  • Tower radio calling convoy and lander slots
  • Suited workers who flee, take cover or surrender
Build

The full population is simulated as numbers, and only the nearest 20–40 people are rendered, using Godot's MultiMesh and animation LOD. That needs three suit variants per faction, rigged with about ten clips each: walk, work, carry, flee, surrender and board vehicle. Rigging is outsourced. Act 1 needs the mining base's crew and tower. The full population system arrives in Update 1.

The city, from your reference image. Pressurised blocks, domes and towers sit on a street grid, linked by tube corridors. Hex-panelled greenhouse domes are spread through the blocks. At the edge, a launch field with long straight roads runs out to the outer pads, and a solar field fills the far side.

Where. Arcadia Planitia, at the real candidate sites studied for Starship landings. Researchers call it one of the few regions where abundant shallow ice is present at relatively low latitude (GeekWire). A 2025 study puts the ice table at roughly 8–12 cm deep in parts of the region (JGR Planets). That is why the city is rich in cells, and why everyone wants it.

How you fight there. Thousands of civilians live in the pressurised core. Firing into a block decompresses it, which causes casualties and costs you reputation. So city missions are about the edges: the launch field, the solar field, the ice mine and the tower. Take those and the city has to deal with you.

Rendering a city

Build it from a modular kit of about 25 pieces: hab block, dome, greenhouse, tower, straight, T and X corridors, pad, and a solar row. The pieces are placed on the street grid by rules, then hand-dressed.

Repeated pieces are drawn with MultiMesh instancing. Distant blocks merge into combined low-detail meshes. Lights in the city windows follow the real day/night cycle. This is the biggest single job for the two rendering seats.

08 · This browser edition

A prototype lab with a hand-off rule

Features are tested here first because iteration is quick. When one is proven, it gets a port card: a short spec, its tuning numbers, a reference capture and the acceptance test. Only port cards go into the Godot backlog. Nothing is copied over as code.

Proven and ready to port: the hijack loop and GASHR, the MOLA horizon, the real sky and day/night cycle, the methalox cell economy, the Level 1 storyboard and the settings screen. Next to prototype here, from the research report: trauma-based camera shake with hit-stop, and the field log and scanner. After those, the flight model, the cell power split and the orbital EVA hijack.

Definition of done, every piece
  • Every frame holds up as a still next to real Mars photos
  • Fully playable with a controller, with correct glyphs
  • 40 fps on the Deck preset
  • No errors in the log, and the save loads
  • Five playtesters finish it without help
